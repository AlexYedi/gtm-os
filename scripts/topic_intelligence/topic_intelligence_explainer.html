The problem01

Event recaps don't compound

Every good event produces insight — a demo that lands, a room that's suddenly all talking about the same thing, a person who keeps showing up at the interesting sessions. But that insight lives in a recap doc and then in nobody's memory. It doesn't accumulate, and it can't be queried.

Topic intelligence treats the whole corpus of events as one system instead of a stack of write-ups. It sits on the already-ingested event graph and asks two questions a recap can't: which themes are gaining or losing altitude, and which distinct themes keep intersecting — in the sessions, and in the people who speak across them. The output feeds two things: differentiated content (a point of view no attendee-with-a-notebook can assemble), and relationship targeting (who to know at a converging intersection).

It's a modeling layer, not a scraper — no new data source. It computes entirely over the graph the events pipeline already populated.

How it works02

Four stages, run every night

Stage 1 — the graph. Events, topics, people, and their relationships, already ingested from the events pipeline. Nothing new is fetched.

Stage 2 — canonicalization. 170 raw topics are non-destructively rolled up into 30 canonical themes. The count wasn't guessed — clustering ran at 20, 30, and 40 and the winner was chosen from a calibration report on intersection density (30 landed at 0.274, the selective middle band: fine enough to be specific, coarse enough that intersections still mean something). Distinct theses stay distinct — "Agentic Commerce", "x402 payment rails", and "A2A transactions" are three topics under one theme, never fused.

Stage 3 — three computations, per window. For each theme, over rolling month / week / all-time windows: Trend compares this window's event volume to the one before it and labels the momentum (heating / cooling / steady / new). Co-occurrence counts theme pairs that share an event. Bridges counts the people who speak across two themes — the connectors.

Stage 4 — snapshots. Each run replaces the current day's rows (a delete-by-date reload, in one transaction) rather than blindly upserting — otherwise a recompute that finds fewer intersections would leave stale pairs behind, reading as real signal. Append-only across days means the trajectory accrues: not just "hot today," but "heating for three weeks."

The payoff03

The connections you can't see from the room

Volume is the boring half. Agent Architecture & Memory led the calendar (12 events), Agent Reliability close behind (11) — the stretch where the question shifted from "can agents work?" to "can we trust them in production?" No surprise if you were there.

The intersections are where it earns its keep — and the strongest ones are carried by shared people, not shared sessions. The top link in the corpus isn't two themes that share a stage; it's two themes that share a crowd:

Where V1 stops04

Honest limits, stated on purpose

At this size the reads are directional, not statistical. Naming exactly where V1 ends is the point — a model you can't see the edges of is a model you can't trust.

Limitation

Thin corpus

59 events. Trends point a direction; they don't forecast. Min-count guards suppress momentum on tiny bases, and low-confidence rows are flagged, not hidden.

Limitation

Windows go quiet when ingestion lags

The rolling month/week windows are computed against today. With the corpus paused at mid-July, the last-7-days window is empty and all-time is the live view. The trend layer is armed, waiting on fresh events — not faking motion on a frozen graph.

Limitation

Bridges over-count

A single event tagged with two themes makes all its speakers "bridge" that pair. So bridge counts correlate with co-occurrence rather than isolating the genuine cross-event connector. Known, and instrumented.

Limitation

One theme per topic

A genuinely bi-thematic topic is filed under a single theme. Cluster-level only; topic-level and embedding-based assignment are deferred until volume demands them.

What's next05

The roadmap is the limitations, inverted

Every V1 shortcut is instrumented with a tripwire — a metric that flips when the simplification stops being good enough. The roadmap isn't a wishlist; it's the set of promotions those tripwires will call for.

Next

Resume ingestion → wake the trend

Fresh events refill the month/week windows and the heating/cooling story goes live. A daily keep-warm heartbeat already protects the snapshot history from gaps.

Next

True cross-event bridges

Separate the connector who spoke at two different theme events from same-event speakers — sharpening "who to know" before it drives outreach.

Later

Multi-membership & embeddings

Let a topic belong to more than one theme, and swap LLM canonicalization for vector assignment once topic volume outpaces cheap classification.

Later

Fire the discrete signal

Emit a topic_intersection signal when a genuinely new pair crosses a threshold, with a suppression-gated public view of the bridge people.
